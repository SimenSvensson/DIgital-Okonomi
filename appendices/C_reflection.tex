\section*{Closing Group Reflection}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Closing Group Reflection}

Before prompting AI, we formulated our own initial ideas
and analytical points for each task.
AI then helped us organise stakeholder lists,
compare possible network effects,
develop a first canvas and examine cybersecurity incentives.
Comparing its outputs with our initial reasoning was useful
because it gave us another interpretation to evaluate
rather than a position to adopt automatically.

AI failed in several recurring ways.
It presented assumptions as facts,
including the TSOs' preferences and the identity
of the ``money side''.
It simplified dependencies and treated the alliance
too much like one firm, for example by listing futures
as a Nord Pool revenue stream without evidence
of the revenue split.
It also suggested instruments such as SLAs
and cyber insurance without establishing
whether the alliance uses them.

Our own revisions required further checking.
We initially interpreted the removal of internal Nordic
capacity constraints as meaning that the system price
was independent of TSO data.
Nord Pool's documentation shows that import and export
flows from the coupled area-price calculation
still enter that calculation.
The revised analysis therefore distinguishes
the pricing processes without claiming complete
independence from capacity inputs.

Our own further review identified a classification problem
in the platform map.
Small Participants are a tariff segment
within buyers and sellers, not automatically a separate side.
Futures traders belong to complementary markets,
while data customers purchase an ancillary service.
Applying the course definition consistently required
revising both the text and the figures.

We also made mistakes while correcting the AI output.
Our first system-price correction went too far, and our platform map
treated a tariff group as a separate side.
We learned to check our own revisions as carefully as the original output.
We have preserved the original outputs in Appendix~B
and recorded subsequent corrections in Appendix~A
so that the development of the analysis remains visible.

The main value of AI was speed and structure.
The economic judgement remained the group's responsibility:
identifying the platform boundary,
distinguishing ownership from operational dependence,
and asking who contributes, who controls and who bears losses.
